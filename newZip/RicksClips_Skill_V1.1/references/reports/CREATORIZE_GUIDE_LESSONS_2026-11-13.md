# What the Creatorize guide teaches us (lead, 2026-11-13)

Source: `<HOME>\Downloads\The Town Built Around a Button - Creatorize Guide.pdf` (19 pages, another creator's member guide; read in full as text). It documents one 65.5 s vertical cartoon short made with their tool in 200 minutes over three runs (first run 160.9 min, two fix rounds of 21.7 and 17.7 min).
Format: for each finding HOW they do it, WHY it works, HOW WE do it, and our gap. Method only: their story is not ours to take.

## 1. Acting is built FIRST, detail LAST
- How: five passes in a fixed order. 1 Build (38 min): the cast acting on every word on a basic lit set with depth, opening on the hero's face. "After this pass the story is all there." 2 Camera angles (24 min): the same acting under a new camera. 3 Environment overhaul (40 + 13 min): the real set. 4 Post (8 min). 5 Shading (9 min).
- Why: story and acting faults show after 38 minutes on cheap sets, before any detail is paid for. Each pass keeps what the others made.
- Ours today: sets and props to Rick's detail level first, camera in parallel, acting last. After a whole day the world scores 8.2-8.7 and the acting was never built (100 of 103 poses static).
- Change: stage 6 becomes "acting pass on block sets", shown to CB as an animatic, BEFORE the detailed set build.

## 2. A beat sheet with seconds, exact words, one picture and one action ON A WORD
- How: 18 beats. Each: start and end second in the voice file, the exact words, what we see, and the action on a named word ("On 'nobody's allowed' a guard's stare snaps to him and he flinches"). The tool turns it into a binding shot plan (34 shots) before it builds anything.
- Why: the picture and the acting are decided in words where a change costs nothing; the same list is the checklist afterwards.
- Ours: SCRIPT.md has a picture sentence per line, but the camera plan was not bound to it and the first storyboard showed 1 of 17.
- Change: the shot table is generated from the beat sheet and the plan check fails a line whose doer, thing and action-on-word are missing (already written into CB_RULES section 0 today as PICTURE SENTENCE CHECK; this gives it its form).

## 3. Read the voice line by line against the frames, one frame per second
- How: after the export, for every line ask "do I see what it says?". It found four lines said but not shown ("he lifts the glass" was never on screen through two exports, with every check passing). "Pretty frames hide story bugs."
- Why: it is the silent test done mechanically; no score or gate replaces it.
- Ours: the judge scores stills in 12 categories against Rick frames; the story count came out only as one remark.
- Change: a LINE CHECK sheet (one row per line: the words, frames at 1 per second, yes or no) is made before any judge round and goes to CB with every animatic.

## 4. A whole-film sheet after every pass, fix the worst thing first
- How: one frame per shot on one sheet after each pass; the worst thing is fixed before moving on.
- Ours: sheets per department, a full storyboard only at the end of a round.
- Change: one film sheet after every pass, looked at by the lead.

## 5. Time budgets
- How: 3 h 20 min for the whole run "for quality, not for speed", a limit per pass, a limit per fix round ("35 minutes at most"); the driver holds the builder to them ("You are 9 minutes over this pass's limit ... write up now").
- Ours: no limits; agents ran 60-150 minutes each, four fix rounds.
- Change: every brief carries a time limit and the lead enforces it.

## 6. The fix message
- How: praise what is good, "keep all of that exactly as it is", then numbered fixes, most important first, each named by its seconds, what is wrong, what it must look like, an "if time is left" item, "nothing else changes", a time limit.
- Ours: long fix lists per department with everything at once.
- Change: fix briefs in this form, at most five items.

## 7. One builder, one conversation
- How: one driver and one builder (plus a crew of three only inside the environment pass); every fix goes into the same conversation, so the whole history is known.
- Ours: a fresh agent per round and per department, each re-reading 40,000 tokens of rules and none knowing the last round.
- Change to consider: fewer, longer-lived agents; resume the same agent for a fix round.

## 8. Motion rules they state
- "Every character acts on the words: faces that snap, one clear body move per line, then a hold. No one just stands there."
- "No shot where nothing moves except the planned stillness."
- These match CB's own rules (studio motion, dead still in holds). They enforce them in the first pass; we did not.

## 9. Close-ups of the twist are the weakest frames
- How: their three scratch close-ups (the story's twist) were the flattest frames and became fix round 1. "Close-ups of one detail get less love than wide shots. Check them first."
- Ours: the same happened: the pin macro, the mugs, the counting eye were our lowest scores.
- Change: the twist and proof close-ups are built and checked first.

## 10. Other points
- One colour per hero, everyone else muted (we have this: grey hero, red pins).
- "Open on a face, never on an empty scene or a back."
- Bright, high-key look asked for in words with a test: "if a frame looks dim next to a bright animated video, it is not done."
- Depth in every shot: something close, the characters, a place far behind.
- A foreground blur with nothing recognisable reads as a smudge: a real object or nothing.
- Exact numbers that matter to the story are spelled out and counted on the frame (47 and 48 scratches). Ours: forty pins, twelve out, nine back.
- Ask for an honest "still weak" list at the end.
- Their voice rule is the opposite of ours: the finished voice is used exactly as it is, pauses included; our voice_prep trims pauses. Not a fault on either side; worth a test.

## Where we are ahead (our creativity gap to keep)
A locked 2D cast with views, outfits and props in real fists; a real hero model (our bomber); a measured reference library; effects on the word; the story-driven world lists. Their houses are one script's kit and their effects are post only.

## Their numbers
160.9 min first run; 200.3 min with both fix rounds. 127.8 million tokens in total, of which 123.3 million were cached re-reads and 370,898 were written output. 33 generated pictures for textures. We did not measure our own token use today, so no comparison is claimed.
