# 3D texture style in the three new references (2026-11-08)

Videos: **This Shape Saved Roller Coasters** (63.2 s), **Why Drivers Started Filming The Road** (62.2 s),
**The Pink Prison Cell Experiment** (65.3 s). All 1080x1920, 30 fps. CB's frames in `references/frames/<title>/`
are 5 fps and match the videos frame for frame (316 / 311 / 326).
**Note:** in the Drivers folder, `frame_297.png` to `frame_311.png` are **0-byte files** (the export stopped early).
The measurements here used frames decoded straight from the video, so nothing is missing.

Baseline, measured with the same scripts: 40 frames from 4 older episodes (Spear vs Shield, Job Nobody Wants in Japan,
Every Memory Has a Price, Pirate Cannonballs) and the camera of 3 older videos. Where a metric matches one in
`REMEASURE_2026-11-05.md`, that number is quoted too.
Private study only. Nothing was uploaded anywhere; nothing in `rick-shorts` or `templates` was changed or rendered.

---

## 0. Bottom line

He has changed the **world**, not the **characters**.

- **Characters: unchanged.** Same flat 2D toon figures: white head, dark plum ink outline, noodle limbs, flat fills.
  New detail is small: stitched seams on caps and pockets, and heads now pick up the colour of the scene light
  (the guard's face is blue-grey under the prison skylights).
- **World: a game-engine 3D look instead of painted flat fields.** Real materials (glossy car paint with reflections,
  chrome rims, wet asphalt, galvanised coaster steel, glazed tile, gravel, grass with blades), real-time lighting
  (hard sun shadows, god rays through windows, bloom on lamps and headlights, rain), and **no ink outlines on the
  world**. Foliage stays stylised (painted tree cards, soft cel-like clouds), which keeps it from looking like a photo.
- **Camera: unchanged.** Same bands as motion.md §2.1 (hold ~6-21 %, move ~55-77 %, p90 18-25 px/f).
- **Grade: darker, cooler, deeper blacks.** The warm orange/amber share collapses and blue/cyan takes over.

In one line: *flat toon characters standing in a stylised-PBR game world, lit like a real-time engine.*

## 2. Materials, texture, grain (by eye on full-res crops, backed by §1)

### 2.1 Surfaces are now real materials, not painted fields
- **Car paint:** glossy clear-coat with environment reflections and a sharp specular streak; chrome/alloy rims; glass
  that shows the interior. Emissive headlights and LED bars with bloom.
- **Asphalt / concrete:** fine noisy albedo (aggregate), painted lane lines as decals, wet-road reflections in the rain scene.
- **Coaster steel:** lattice girders with real thickness and cast shadows; galvanised grey with small highlights.
- **Prison:** glazed concrete block with mortar joints in geometry/normal map, polished floor with soft reflections,
  steel doors with panel detail; the pink cell is one saturated matte paint over the same block.
- **Ground:** grass is a textured terrain with scattered blades/clumps and painted dirt decals (orange patches).
- **Gravel roof (aerial):** extreme fine noise that **aliases and shimmers** when the camera moves. This is a weakness
  in his render, not something to copy.

### 2.2 What stays stylised
- Trees: stylised foliage cards with soft painted shading (not photo trees).
- Clouds: soft white cel-like puffs with a light grey underside.
- Sky: smooth painted gradient (day blue, dusk violet-pink in Drivers).
- Props with text/signage stay clean and readable.

### 2.3 Grain
None. Residual noise in smooth areas is 0.13-0.15 grey levels in both old and new (h264 smoothing). The "texture"
feeling comes from **surface albedo detail and speculars**, not from a film-grain overlay. Do not add grain.

### 2.4 Ink
- **Characters keep ink** exactly as before (not re-measured here; REMEASURE's #231218, 6.9 % W stands).
- **The world has no outlines.** The Coaster and Drivers stroke counts are high only because thin dark *geometry*
  (lattice, rails, tyres, window frames) reads like ink to the detector. Pink, whose world has few thin dark parts,
  gives the honest number: 38 % of strong edges near a dark stroke, against 59 % in the older episodes with the same tool.
  By eye, cars, walls, doors, tracks and trees carry **no drawn line**.
- So the line contrast is now **character vs world**: inked toon figures pop off an un-inked 3D set.

## 3. Lighting and shading

- **Physically lit world:** a strong key (sun or skylights) with **hard cast shadows** of windows, railings and
  characters on the floor; soft ambient fill; ambient occlusion in corners.
- **Volumetrics:** god rays through windows (prison), sun shafts in the haze (Drivers suburb), rain streaks and mist
  on the highway.
- **Bloom:** lamps, headlights, string lights on the coaster, the lit loop at night. Clipped pixels 3x the old rate.
- **Aerial perspective:** distant hills and trees fade to blue-grey haze in the wide shots.
- **Characters are lit by the scene:** cast shadows on the floor, and head/clothing tint toward the key colour
  (cool blue in the prison, pink in the cell, warm at dusk). Their own shading stays flat (one soft gradient).
- **Night:** deep navy, near-black shadows, moon + warm practical lights. Blacks go to (0-7, 0-6, 6-15).

## 4. Palette

- Cooler and darker than the old look: Y′ mean 0.46 vs 0.54, blue/cyan 58 % of saturated pixels, warm 7 %.
- Shadows blue (b* −8), not plum-magenta (a* +6 vs +12.5).
- One dominant accent per episode: red/orange (Coaster), red + teal cars (Drivers), hot pink (Pink, 34 % of saturated pixels).
- Lit C* lower (21 vs 28-32): surfaces are more naturalistic; saturation lives in the accent objects and the sky.

## 6. What this changes for the build (recommendations, nothing applied)

Ranked by visual impact. For the Blender pipeline (Night Witches rebuild and later).

1. **Drop Freestyle ink on the environment; keep it on characters only.** This is the single biggest change, and it
   also removes the CPU-bound Freestyle cost on the whole scene (put sets on a view layer / line set that excludes them).
2. **Real PBR materials on hero props:** glossy clear-coat for vehicles, metal with roughness 0.25-0.4, glazed tile,
   wet asphalt; rely on albedo/normal detail for "texture", not on a grain overlay.
3. **Light like an engine:** one strong sun/area key with hard shadows, volumetric shafts in interiors, bloom on
   practicals, haze in wide shots. Let characters receive scene light colour and cast shadows.
4. **Grade cooler and deeper:** black point toward (7,4,15) instead of (28,13,20); shadows blue (b* ≈ −8, a* ≈ +6);
   one saturated accent colour per episode on a cool base; vignette corner/centre ≈ 0.67.
5. **Keep foliage, clouds and sky stylised** so the world stays "toon-adjacent", and keep characters exactly as specified.
6. **Avoid his aliasing:** fine high-frequency textures (gravel) need mipmapping / higher samples, or they shimmer.
7. **Camera: no change** from motion.md §2.1. Add POV/vehicle-mounted and aerial angles where the story allows.

**Conflicts with existing specs:** textures.md ("every surface as a few flat colour fields", thin painted lines) and
outlines.md (40-45 % of strong edges inked) describe the older look. For the new 3D style, treat those as superseded
for environments; the character specs (characters.md, REMEASURE §2) are unchanged.

## 7. Caveats

- The old baseline here is a small sample (40 frames, 4 episodes); REMEASURE's 3,864-frame numbers are quoted beside it
  where the definitions match. My vignette uses corner boxes, so it is not identical to grade.md's.
- Material descriptions in §2-3 are by eye on full-resolution crops; the numbers in §1 support the direction, not
  per-material albedos (those would need hand boxes, as in textures.md).
- Character head metrics (fill luma, gradient) were not re-measured on the new videos.

## 8. How the characters are made (added 2026-11-08, after CB: "I think it is a mix of coded and 3D")

CB is right: it is a **hybrid**. The world is 3D; the characters are **2D vector drawings (the same flat, coded-looking
style as before) placed as flat cut-out cards inside the 3D scene**, not 3D models. Evidence, frame by frame:
- **Heads stay perfect circles/beans from every camera angle** (high-angle cell shot Pink f235, low car-interior Drivers f1):
  a 3D head would foreshorten; a camera-facing card does not.
- **3D objects pass in front of them:** benches and tables cut across legs (Pink f170/183), car seats and the steering
  wheel overlap the driver (Drivers f1, f261). So the cards sit at a real depth in the 3D scene, not pasted on top afterwards.
- **They ride with 3D objects:** the coaster rider hangs upside down with the car (Coaster f287); the card is parented to it.
- **Poses are drawn for the shot:** seated with legs to the pedals, seat-belt across the chest, hand on the wheel,
  sitting on a bench edge. Custom 2D poses per beat, then placed in 3D.
- **Light only tints them** (face-fill samples, median of the bright pixels):
  | scene | face fill RGB |
  |---|---|
  | day coaster, car interior | (255,248,250), (253,249,243): white |
  | **night coaster** | **(251,245,233): still white at night** |
  | blue prison hall, in shade | (151,152,168): dimmed + cool |
  | rain highway | (215,220,225): cool grey |
  | **pink cell** | **(232,191,214): tinted pink** |
  So the cards are mostly **self-lit** (they stay readable at night) but pick up the colour of strong coloured light
  and darken in shade. They are not fully lit like the 3D world.
- **Screen devices on top:** CCTV scan-lines over a cell shot (Pink f235), the dash-cam overlay, the captions.

**For our build:** draw characters with OUR system (toon.ts Pen drawings, the 4 views, bean head h/w 0.93, plum ink #231218),
export them as transparent cards, place them in Blender as camera-facing cards at real depth (so props can overlap
them), parent them to vehicles, and shade the card as about half emission + half lit, so they stay bright at night and
still take a tint from coloured light.
