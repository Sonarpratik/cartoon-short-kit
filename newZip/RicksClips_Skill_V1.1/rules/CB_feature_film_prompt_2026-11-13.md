what can we learn from here that we dont have ? Create a 3D animated short film for the web. It must look like a still-by-still excerpt from a finished theatrical animated feature: painterly, glowing storybook 3D with rich, realistic materials. Not a tech demo, not a game scene, not low-poly art.
      
      Deliver two things: a web page that plays the film in real time, and a 1920x1080, 30 fps MP4 rendered frame by frame from the same code, with a fully synthesized soundtrack. Pure three.js. No video model, no image model, no external assets, no audio samples. Use any tools, libraries, references and guides you want, and split the work across parallel agents if it helps (hero, one world each, optics, sound).
      
      The hero is an original character: a little boy astronaut in an oversized cream retro spacesuit with orange accents, a glass fishbowl helmet, tousled copper-red hair and a brass spyglass. He rides in a wicker basket (sandbags, a hooked lantern, coiled rope) under a patched, striped storybook hot-air balloon in cream, faded red, mustard and dusty teal. Pennants and long ribbons trail behind. When the burner fires, the whole balloon glows from inside like a paper lantern. That warm glow is the one constant light of the film. No dialogue and no text on screen.
      
      SCENE
      
      One journey through eight worlds, from sunset through night to dawn. Each world gets two shots of about six seconds: a wide establishing shot with the balloon small against something huge, then a close or contrasting angle. Dip to black between worlds. About 100 seconds in total.
      
      1. Sea of Clouds. Sunset above an endless sea of clouds. A sky whale breaches in a slow arc across the low sun; another glides beside the basket.
      2. Valley of Kites. A rose-crimson mountain pass of sandstone spires with cliff dwellings and a great rock arch. Hundreds of kites, prayer-flag lines and silk ribbons; a long dragon kite undulates past the basket. The boy waves.
      3. Sky Lanterns. Blue hour over a sleeping river town. Thousands of paper lanterns rise around the balloon, the near ones with visible paper, wire and flame.
      4. Storm Cathedral. Night inside a thunderstorm. Colossal cloud walls form a nave; lightning flickers inside the clouds; rain streaks past; the burner roars and the balloon is the only warm light.
      5. Moon Islands. Calm after the storm. A giant moon, floating rock islands with lit windows, waterfalls and a rope bridge, a lighthouse sweeping its beam.
      6. Ice Fjord. Polar night. Glacier walls, an ice arch, and mirror-black water that doubles the green aurora and the glowing balloon.
      7. River of Stars. Above the sky itself. A flowing river of stardust, nebulae, a ringed planet, a comet. The balloon sails into the stream.
      8. Dawn. Pastel sunrise over a patchwork land. Dozens of other balloons rise out of the mist to meet him. He lowers the spyglass, turns and waves, and the frame washes to warm light, then fades to black.
      
      FILM QUALITY STANDARD
      
      Every frame must look as if it came from a finished animated feature, lit by a cinematographer and dressed by a set designer.
      
      Hero: Storybook proportions with real volume and charm. Procedural materials with visible detail: fabric weave, stitched patches, wicker, leather, brass. A simple, appealing face with glossy eyes. He looks around, raises the spyglass, waves; the basket lags the balloon as it sways. On-model and readable from every angle, even when tiny in frame.
      
      Worlds: One dominant colour palette per world and a few large, iconic set pieces with strong silhouettes. Deep layered space: big foreground shapes, the hero in the mid-ground, far shapes dissolving into coloured haze. Detail at three scales, so a frame rewards a second look: bridges, ladders, lit windows, chimney smoke, creatures.
      
      Materials: Noise-displaced, smooth-shaded geometry with procedural colour and bump textures for rock, ice, cloud, paper and cloth. Rim light from each world's key light. Soft contact with mist and cloud.
      
      Particles: At least two layers in every shot. Tiny motes near the lens that catch the light, plus each world's own: ice crystals, petals, sparks, rain, stardust, snow, dandelion seeds. Small, soft, numerous and twinkling.
      
      Lighting: Each world has one clear key light. The burner is always the only warm note in cold worlds and never clips to white. Glow and bloom are soft, never blown out.
      
      Lens: A real camera. Depth of field with the hero crisp and the background melting into round bokeh, gentle halation, a faint anamorphic streak on the brightest points, fine film grain.
      
      Animation: Slow, steady camera moves. Nothing is static: ribbons flutter, clouds drift, water ripples, lights flicker. Everything is a pure function of time so any frame can be rendered exactly.
      
      Sound: A tender orchestral lullaby (felt piano, warm strings, celesta, harp) with one memorable theme that is stated at sunset, breaks into minor in the storm and returns in full at dawn, landing its final chord on the boy's wave. Add wind, whale song, wooden chimes and cloth, a distant bell, rain and thunder after each lightning flash, the burner's roar, ice creaks, starry sparkles and a dawn chorus, all timed to the picture and mastered to about -16 LUFS.
      
      Direction: The pacing starts warm and spacious, turns playful, hushes, hits a dramatic low point in the storm, opens into wonder among the stars, and ends in a joyful dawn where he is no longer alone.
      
      IT MUST NOT LOOK LIKE
      
      - A tech demo: No empty scenes, default lighting, visible primitives or flat untextured surfaces.
      - Low-poly or blocky art: No faceted rocks, cone trees or toy-like boxes.
      - A video game: No HUD feel, tiling textures, pop-in or hard-edged sprites.
      - Flat cards: Clouds, smoke and mist must feel soft and voluminous, never like billboards.
      - Web animation: No constant bobbing, sliding layers or smooth software easing on everything.
      - AI video: No warped shapes, flicker, melting details or a hero whose design changes between shots.
      - Amateur work: No blown-out highlights, muddy colours, objects cut by the frame edge, or things clipping through the camera.
      
      You are free to change any part of this prompt if it gives a better result.
      
      The result should feel tender, wondrous and warm, like a bedtime story you remember forever. Before finishing, render random frames as still images and inspect them. If any frame would not pass as a still from a theatrical animated film, keep improving it. Do not stop until every frame does.
