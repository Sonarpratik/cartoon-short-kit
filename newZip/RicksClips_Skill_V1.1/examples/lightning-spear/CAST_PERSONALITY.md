# CAST PERSONALITY: Why The Safest Tower Had A Spear On Top (writer, 2026-11-15; CB_RULES section 5, the 13 points)

Four roles plus a crowd and one hen: the Mason (hero), the Priest (the kind mocker and boss), the Rival (the perfect builder), the Bell Ringer, the townsfolk, the white hen.
Everyone is unnamed and invented. Preset IDs: the character-artist assigns them. Bodies from the three-size bible; SHORT COSTUMES BY DEFAULT, era northern Italy about 1765 (knee breeches, stockings, buckle shoes, waistcoats; no long coats). The Priest's cassock is the one allowed long cloth.
Period check done by the writer (hat, neckwear, facial hair): men of the 1760s are CLEAN-SHAVEN (no moustaches, no beards, no sideburns); hats are tricornes for gentlemen and soft caps for workmen (no top hats, no flat caps with a peak); neckwear is a knotted neckerchief for workmen and a white stock or lace jabot for gentlemen (no ties); wigs with a black ribbon at the back for the rich only.
Contrast comedy for the whole film: one quiet young man who does one plain ugly thing and waits, among a town that trusts whatever is tallest, whitest and loudest. The kind worrier (Priest) is wrong and changes; the proud expert (Rival) is wrong and loses; the brave believer (Bell Ringer) runs and is right to.

---

## THE MASON (hero; STANDARD body, young, about 20)
Look at first sight: calm, practical, sleeves rolled, stone dust on him. Soft brown workman's cap, short dark hair tied in a small tail at the neck, clean-shaven, cream shirt with rolled sleeves, short leather apron to mid-thigh, grey knee breeches, grey stockings, hammer in a belt loop.
**Signature detail (fixed, every view): a mustard-yellow neckerchief knotted to the LEFT, and one white chalk handprint on the leather apron.**
1. Temperament: quiet, patient, stubborn without anger.
2. Confidence vs skill: both high; he shows neither. He looks like the boy who carries the stones.
3. Fears: the storm arriving before the wire's foot is in the ground.
4. Motivation: that the tower he works on is still standing in the morning.
5. Emotional habits: when laughed at he looks at the sky, not at the people; when proved right he does not gloat, he checks the wire.
6. Quirks: licks a finger and holds it to the wind; measures everything against his thumb; tugs a thing once to test it.
7. Posture: upright, weight even, head a little to one side when he listens.
8. Movement rhythm: steady and economical; quick only with the hammer.
9. Signature gestures (5): (a) hammer swing with the other fist on the iron; (b) thumb held beside the wire; (c) one firm tug on the wire; (d) finger to the wind, glance at the cloud; (e) one arm out to keep someone back from the wire's foot.
10. Reaction speed: SLOW and small; he has already thought of it.
11. Relationships: to the Priest, a respectful worker who will not obey on this one thing; to the Rival, none (he never looks at the white tower); to the hen, a patient neighbour.
12. Status: the town fool at noon, forgotten in spring and summer, the only one who understood on the autumn night, and by dawn the man with the most work in town. He does not change.
13. Never speaks. His answer to both quotes is a look at the spear.

## THE PRIEST (the mocker and boss; FAT body, about 60)
Look at first sight: round, kind, fussy, pink cheeks, worried eyebrows. Black cassock (long, allowed) with a row of small buttons and a black sash, small white collar, wide round black hat with a low crown, clean-shaven, a ring of white hair under the hat, buckle shoes.
**Signature detail (fixed, every view): a big wooden rosary at the sash, on his RIGHT side, that he grabs whenever he is worried.**
1. Temperament: warm, anxious, fatherly; quick to fear, quick to forgive.
2. Confidence vs skill: sure of what everybody has always known (bells), no idea about iron; honest enough to change his mind in one night.
3. Fears: his church burning; being the priest whose tower fell.
4. Motivation: to keep his building and his people safe. He is never a fool of faith: he is a careful owner with the knowledge of his time.
5. Emotional habits: fear goes to his hands (rosary, shielding a candle); relief makes him generous (the pat on the shoulder).
6. Quirks: counts seconds on the rosary beads; shields the nearest candle with his hand; both chins jump on a strong word.
7. Posture: belly forward, head pulled a little into the shoulders, hands together at the sash.
8. Movement rhythm: small quick steps, a trot; sudden stops.
9. Signature gestures (5): (a) both fists to the rosary; (b) one fist pointing straight up at his ceiling; (c) a hand cupped round a candle flame; (d) the kind pat on the Mason's shoulder with a nod toward the bell; (e) a small blessing over the armful of spears.
10. Reaction speed: FAST and big (the opposite of the Mason).
11. Relationships: to the Mason, an alarmed but fond boss, then a kind winner, then his best customer; to the Bell Ringer, shelter under his porch; to the Rival, a polite neighbour.
12. Status: the owner who knows best (dusk), the one proved right (spring), a frightened man behind the Mason's arm (autumn), and at dawn the happiest convert in town, holding the ladder.
13. SPEAKS rows 5 and 12. Row 5: alarm, eyes up to the ceiling and back, head ducked. Row 12: warm, pleased, eyes on the Mason, nod toward the bell.

## THE RIVAL (the perfect builder; THIN body, tall, about 45)
Look at first sight: elegant, pleased with himself, chin up. Black tricorne, small white powdered wig with a black ribbon bow at the neck, clean-shaven, white lace jabot, short sky-blue waistcoat with gold buttons over white shirt sleeves, cream knee breeches, white stockings, buckle shoes.
**Signature detail (fixed, every view): a long measuring cane with a gold knob, always in his RIGHT fist.**
1. Temperament: proud, exact, a showman of good taste.
2. Confidence vs skill: very high confidence, real skill in stone and beauty, none in weather.
3. Fears: a fingerprint on his marble.
4. Motivation: to own the finest tower in the land and be seen owning it.
5. Emotional habits: admiration makes him bow; a flaw makes him polish.
6. Quirks: wipes marble with his cuff; points with the cane, never with a finger.
7. Posture: very straight, weight on one leg, cane planted.
8. Movement rhythm: smooth, slow, drawn out; every move ends in a pose.
9. Signature gestures (4): (a) the bow with the cane held wide; (b) cuff wiping a spot; (c) cane pointing up at the angel; (d) the frozen stare with the hat sliding off (row 21).
10. Reaction speed: late; he needs a moment to believe a bad thing.
11. Relationships: to the town, a host; to the Bell Ringer, an employer who, when it counts, puts an arm out to keep him back from the fire; to the Mason, he has never noticed him.
12. Status: the winner all through spring and summer, an onlooker in the square in autumn. He is not punished further: safe, hatless, silent.
13. Never speaks.

## THE BELL RINGER (THIN body, short, about 30)
Look at first sight: small, wiry, earnest, big ears. Soft grey knitted cap, clean-shaven, collarless shirt, short brown waistcoat, patched knee breeches, bare calves, wooden clogs.
**Signature detail (fixed, every view): a tuft of white wool sticking out of each ear.**
1. Temperament: dutiful, brave on the outside, nervous underneath.
2. Confidence vs skill: an excellent ringer, total trust in the bell.
3. Fears: letting the town down; the white light in the arches.
4. Motivation: to ring the storm away, because that is his job.
5. Emotional habits: fear makes him pull harder, until the night it makes him run.
6. Quirks: pushes the wool deeper before each pull; taps the bell with a knuckle like knocking on a door.
7. Posture: bent knees, both fists high on the rope.
8. Movement rhythm: big rhythmic pulls; when he runs, tiny fast steps.
9. Signature gestures (4): (a) the two-fisted haul; (b) wool check; (c) legs pedalling in the air on the rope; (d) the look at hand, arch, hand before he lets go.
10. Reaction speed: instant.
11. Relationships: proud of the Rival's bell; ends under the Priest's porch.
12. Status: the town's protector (spring), its hero (summer), the first to understand with his legs (autumn). He is out of the tower and far down the road before the bolt; he is seen safe in row 21.
13. Never speaks.

## THE TOWNSFOLK (mixed bodies)
Market people about 1765: women in kerchiefs, laced bodices and aprons in ochre, green and madder; men in caps or plain tricornes, waistcoats and breeches, all clean-shaven. Team colours: the market wears the awnings' red and white somewhere (a ribbon, a striped apron). Fixed figures: the baker with his breadsticks (ten, five, one), the olive seller with the toothpick, one boy who eats the olive.
They move as one: laugh together, count together, climb together. Status: the judges at noon, the counters in every storm, silent in the square at the end.

## THE WHITE HEN
Round, white, cross. **Signature detail: ONE black tail feather.** She owns the warm spot at the foot of the wire (row 3), is blown into a ball there (row 19) and sits beside the hero on her coop in the last picture, the one roof-dweller who does not hop away. She is never hurt, only offended.
