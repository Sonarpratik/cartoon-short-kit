# sources.md: lightning-spear (2026-11-15)

OPENED = page fetched and the supporting sentence read. SNIPPET = search summary only, not to be cited alone. Wikipedia-only items are marked.

## Opened

1. Franklin, Poor Richard Improved 1753 ("How to secure Houses, &c. from Lightning"), Library of America text copy: https://usinfo.org/usia/usinfo.state.gov/usa/infousa/facts/loa/bf1753.htm
   - "Provide a small Iron Rod (it may be made of the Rod-iron used by the Nailers)"; one end "three or four Feet in the moist Ground"; the other "six or eight Feet above the highest Part of the Building"; "secured to the House by a few small Staples"; a point sharpened to "a fine Point"; "a middling Wire along the Ridge".
   - Founders Online original (page returned empty to the fetch tool): https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Franklin/01-04-02-0148
2. Wikipedia, Lightning rod (Wikipedia only): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_rod
   - "conceived by Benjamin Franklin in 1749"; goal "a sufficiently low-impedance path for lightning to travel to ground"; lightning "is most likely to strike the rod and be conducted to ground through a wire"; British scientists wanted a ball on the end, American scientists a point.
3. Wikipedia, Bell-ringer (Wikipedia only): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell-ringer
   - "it was believed that the sound of a bell could disperse thunder"; "103 bell-ringers were killed during thunderstorms" (France 1753-1786); "The Parlement of Paris enforced an edict in 1786 to prohibit the practice."
4. Wikipedia, Speed of sound (Wikipedia only): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound
   - Mersenne 1630 (flash to bang with a seconds pendulum, about 448 m/s); Borelli and Viviani, Accademia del Cimento, 1650, about 350 m/s; Derham 1708/1709, flash to bang with a half-second pendulum.
5. Wikipedia, Giuseppe Toaldo (Wikipedia only): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Toaldo
   - Toaldo "advocated the erection of lightning rods" on Franklin's design; 1772 treatise and 1774 pamphlet; conductors placed on Siena Cathedral, St Mark's tower, powder magazines and Venetian ships (no dates given on the page).
6. Basilica di San Marco, official site, Bell tower: https://www.basilicasanmarco.it/en/campanile/
   - "irresistible attraction for lightning"; 1489 top "torn off by a bolt of lightning"; 1745 strike "tore away the corner facing the Clock Tower"; "on 18 May 1776, Abbot Giuseppe Toaldo" placed a lightning rod; "lightning strikes no longer caused any damage"; 1513 gilded-copper angel weathervane.
7. Images of Venice, St Mark's Campanile: https://imagesofvenice.com/st-marks-campanile/
   - 1489: "lightning again struck the tower ... setting ablaze the spire", bells fell, "the masonry of the tower itself cracked"; damage "mostly due to lightning strikes on the top wooden structures"; "18 March 1776 ... Giuseppe Toaldo ... installed a lightning rod; the first in Venice."
8. Science Museum Group, iron bar from the 1769 St Paul's conductor: https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co53931/section-of-lightning-conductor-from-st-pauls-cathedral
   - "Iron bar which formed one section of the lightning conductor installed on St. Paul's cathedral, London, in 1769"; 50 x 660 x 135 mm, 10 kg.
