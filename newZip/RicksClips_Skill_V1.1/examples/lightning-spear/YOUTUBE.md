## Description
In a town terrified of lightning, one young mason climbed the church tower and nailed an iron spear to the top. ⚡ Then he ran an iron wire, thick as a thumb, all the way down into the mud. The priest went pale. The whole market laughed at his "toothpick tower" and went to admire the rival's: taller, white marble, a gold angel and the biggest storm bell in the land.

Then the storms came. Ten seconds between flash and thunder. Then five. Then one…

You see, lightning looks for the easiest road to the ground. The spear and the wire were that road, and the bolt ran right past the tower into the wet earth. The perfect tower had no road at all.

📜 History note: this is a fable built on a true idea. Benjamin Franklin described the pointed lightning rod in his 1753 almanac: a small iron rod, fixed with staples, its end in moist ground. Church bells really were rung against storms in Europe, and towers were hit again and again: the bell tower of St Mark's in Venice had its wooden top set ablaze by lightning in 1489, and after it got a rod in 1776, no more lightning damage is recorded. The mason, the priest, the rival and the angry chicken are our story.
