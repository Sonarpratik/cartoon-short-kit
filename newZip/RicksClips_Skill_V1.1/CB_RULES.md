## HARD RULE NO. 2: NOTHING GENERIC PASSES. EVERYTHING IS ULTRA DETAILED. (CB 2026-11-11: "Can we add rules hard rule. Nothing generic wont pass. Everything need to be ultra detailed")
This is a gate, not advice. A frame that fails it is not shown to CB and not rendered further.
- **Generic = fail.** A plain box, ball tree, flat one-colour surface, typed font, filler word, star in place of a word, empty prop, standing or floor-sit pose where the story needs a real action, or a set that could belong to any film.
- **Every object in frame is specific to THIS story** and built from real parts, down to the small things: a wallet has pockets, stitching, a photo and flies that fly out; a board carries a real blueprint; a cart carries the ride's name; a loop under construction shows the crane and the missing piece.
- **Scale is checked:** trees about a quarter of the hero structure, a vehicle small around its rider, characters as large in frame as Rick's.
- **Poses are specific:** seated means a chair-sit with knees forward, sleeping means slumped with a tilt; body moves come from the trained motion library.
- **The test before CB sees it:** put the frame beside Rick's and name five specific details in every main object. If you cannot, or if Rick's shows a kind of thing ours lacks, it fails. Fix it first.
Full detail list: section 3, "PREMIUM DETAIL IN EVERYTHING".

## 1. Script and voice
- [ ] Rick script pattern; ALWAYS open on action, never a summary or setup line. ("always start action")
- [ ] Told like a 10-year-old tells a friend; story lines are never cut for length.
- [ ] Voice = Energetic Storyteller, pace 1.0, a mood tag on EVERY line. ("set standard at 1.0", "it sound all too flat")
- [ ] THE FIRST TAKE IS ENERGETIC, AND A QUOTE SOUNDS LIKE ANOTHER PERSON (CB 2026-11-15, lightning spear take 1: "too flat. and when someone say something you need to make a different tone"; the same "too flat" note fell on goldrush take 1). The first voice text already uses two- or three-word energy tags on every clause ("[excited, energetic, fast]", "[dramatic, building up]") and exclamation marks; no bare [soft]. Every quoted line gets a character tone clearly away from the narrator (pitch, pace or attitude: "[panicked, high-pitched, shouting]", "[smug, slow, deep voice]"), still the one narrator voice. Check by numbers before CB hears it: pitch range per sentence against the last approved take, and each quote at least about 2 semitones or a clear pace step away from its neighbours.
- [ ] Captions on, Rick style: Arial Bold white, thin black outline, 1-2 words, 3.9 % of frame height, baseline 71.5 %, no pop.

## 2. Every picture (the mechanism)
- [ ] FRAME 0 IS THE HOOK: a character doing the strange act with the hero object readable. Never an object close-up, at the start or as the loop frame. ("start frame should be something noticeable, a hook")
- [ ] One idea per picture = the words being spoken. Doer + thing + result in the same frame. New phrase = new picture or a clear move.
- [ ] SILENT TEST: the action is acted out, so the story reads with the sound off. ("you understand the work without having the narration tell anything")
- [ ] 2-4 elements, subject big, nothing in front of the subject or the hero object. No empty frames.

- [ ] Exaggerate the idea as a funny prop or scale, not as random effects.

- **PREMIUM DETAIL IN EVERYTHING (CB 2026-11-11, roller coaster: "He got more details in everything. Like the rollercoaster cart, the cars and other environment, the full sets", "Everything looks more premium more detailed. Make it as rule in skill").** Detail is built into the MODEL first, surface patterns come after. A plain box with a wood pattern on it is not detail.
  - **Hero objects and vehicles:** real parts. Boards with a rim, hinges, handles, bolts, a frame and wheels below, a padded seat with a pattern, chrome, tyre tread, plates, lamps. Compare the hero object alone at full size with Rick's before CB sees the frame.
  - **Structures:** thin lattice trusses with cross braces, footings, guy wires with anchors, rust and wear, and the structure's shadow on the ground. No thick plain bars.
  - **Metal is shiny:** rails, chrome and tools catch the light with a bright streak. Impacts throw sparks (hammer on rail), with a flash on the hit frame.

## 5. Movement and acting
- [ ] NEVER A STATIC KIT POSE (CB 2026-11-13 evening, bomber armor animatic: "rules never use static kit pose. we made a rule for movement yesterday night", "no natural body movement, see the fireworks test we did it much better. you forgot something"). The kit's plain poses (stand, hold_out, knock, hold_up, hands_hips, grip_* used as a frozen stance ...) are blocking stand-ins only and never reach a storyboard, an animatic or CB. Every act of every character is a MOVE that plays through time (`m:<id>`): an episode move written for that action, or a trained library move for walks, climbs, kneels and carries (picked by action, feeling, energy and character, with its reason in the shot table), with the way in and the way out acted (walk in, kneel, climb, carry, turn). A prop grip is a library move with the grip on it, never a frozen grip pose. The bomber armor film went to CB with 100 of 103 poses static and not one walk: that is the failure this rule names. GATE (to be built into tools/film_plan.py; until then the lead counts by hand before any storyboard render): fail on any act whose pose is not `m:`, on a frozen move (`@=`), on `walk: true` with the planner's stand-in walk, on a film without `"tween": { "ones": true }`, and on `face_frames` above 0 (faces change as a step). A move the library lacks is designed in three styles and added first.
  CORRECTION (CB 2026-11-13, same evening: "the fireworks test clip. it move from itself. we didnt train it, followed another rule that we made"): natural body movement comes from TWO rules together, and the fireworks test used the second. (1) Trained library moves for the cycles and set pieces (walk, run, climb, kneel, carry ...). (2) STUDIO MOTION for every other action (`cloud_rules/motion/STUDIO_MOTION.md`): the body moves by itself through the action sequence NOTICE, READ, ANTICIPATE, HOLD, STRIKE, IMPACT, REACT, SETTLE, DEAD STILL, on ones, with the snap curve (about 70 % of the move in 5 frames, then a slow settle), the head leading a notice and lagging a strike, hands travelling on arcs, a counter-move before every strike, props and hanging things settling after the hand stops, all solved with BODY MECHANICS (fixed limb lengths, the hand goes to its target and the body follows). A pose change that jumps or blends straight from A to B without that sequence is a static kit pose in disguise and fails the same gate.

- [ ] CHARACTER ACTING, MICRO-EXPRESSIONS, PERSONALITY-DRIVEN PHYSICAL COMEDY (CB 2026-11-13 02:03 Bangkok, pasted as a rule): characters think, feel, anticipate and react; they never just perform a mechanical action. Every significant action carries ONE motivated human behaviour detail that comes from what the character is thinking or doing, never a random add-on: tongue slightly out of one mouth corner while concentrating, one eye squinted while aiming, biting the lower lip while working, one eyebrow up when confused, head tilt while thinking, holding the breath on a delicate task. Use facial asymmetry (brows and eyes move independently), eye direction and attention shifts, reaction delays, small wrist corrections and personality body language (tense shoulders, puffed chest). Write each gag as an ACTING SEQUENCE with times: how the behaviour starts, holds and changes, e.g. drilling 0.0-1.0 s studies the target, head tilt, eyes narrow; 1.0-2.0 leans in, tongue slowly comes out without him noticing; 2.0-3.5 drills, shoulders tense, tiny wrist corrections, brows furrowed; 3.5-4.2 drill jolts, he freezes, eyes wide, tongue still out; 4.2-5.0 slow glance to the camera, tongue back in, embarrassed. Comedy comes from tiny unconscious habits, awkward pauses, overconfidence and unexpected reactions (the nervous smile and pretend-nothing-happened after a mistake; the smug half-smile while pressing the wrong button). Faces sync with the action; no frozen faces, no repeated gestures, no random expressions, no robotic moves. HOW IT FITS OUR RULES: the face still starts calm and the big change lands ON the narration word (EXPRESSIONS); the behaviour detail plays DURING the action and the acting sequence; once a character has settled, DEAD STILL IN HOLDS still wins (blinks only, no idle breathing); the head wobble comes with each expression change. The writer marks the behaviour detail per action in the shot table, the animator acts it, and the character-artist adds any face part the rig lacks (tongue-out mouth, one-eye squint, independent brows, lip bite) as a request to the lead before the shot is built.

The behavior must emerge naturally from what the character is thinking or doing, rather than being added as random animation." The goal is human-like character acting enhanced by stylized cartoon timing and physical comedy; thought-driven acting separates a character who performs an action from one who feels like a funny little human with a personality.
- [ ] UNIVERSAL VISUAL COMEDY AND GAG SYSTEM (CB 2026-11-13 02:05 Bangkok, pasted as a rule; his text kept): Automatically identify opportunities for clever, context-aware visual humor throughout every animation scene. Do not wait for the script to explicitly request a gag. Invent original visual jokes that naturally emerge from the character's goals, personality, actions, props, environment, and current story situation. PRINCIPLES: 1 Situational comedy (jokes from what is happening in the scene, not random events). 2 Visual metaphors (poverty, exhaustion, confidence, stupidity, failure or success turned into clear funny physical images). 3 Prop comedy (ordinary objects get unexpected, exaggerated or absurd behaviour that reinforces the story). 4 Character-driven humor (unconscious habits, quirks, awkward gestures, overconfidence, hesitation, exaggerated reactions; see CHARACTER ACTING). 5 Expectation reversal (set up the obvious, reveal an unexpected but understandable outcome). 6 Escalation (a small problem grows into a bigger comic consequence). 7 Physical comedy (slapstick with believable body mechanics, balance, weight and timing; BODY MECHANICS, CONTACT AND GROUNDING still apply). 8 Background comedy (occasional subtle secondary jokes that reward attention without pulling focus from the main action). 9 Silent storytelling (the gag reads without narration, dialogue or captions). 10 Comedic timing (anticipation, setup, pause, reveal, reaction, recovery; a short stillness sells the punchline). CREATIVE FREEDOM: never repeat the same joke template; choose a different gag mechanism per scene; think like an experienced cartoon storyboard artist. INTEGRATION: only where the gag adds to the scene's meaning, pacing or personality; one strong readable gag beats several weak ones; the story stays coherent, characters stay consistent, essential narration and key information are never interrupted. EXECUTION: for each gag decide setup, anticipation, visual reveal, reaction and exit transition; sell it with micro-expressions, body language, props, secondary motion and camera framing, keeping IK, limb proportions, object contact, collision, ground placement and spatial continuity. QUALITY CONTROL before finalizing: is it understood at once, does it fit this exact scene, is it visually surprising, does it reveal character or reinforce the story, is the timing readable? If not, replace it or drop it. HOW WE RUN IT: the writer proposes one gag per beat in the shot table (mechanism named, so no two scenes reuse one); the camera-director frames the reveal; the animator and vfx-artist time it; the storyboard-judge runs the five quality questions; gags stay in the story's world and period and never copy a Rick gag.
- [ ] UNIVERSAL CHARACTER PERSONALITY, PSYCHOLOGY AND SOCIAL DYNAMICS (CB 2026-11-13 02:11 Bangkok, pasted as a rule; his 13 points kept): act as a character animation director, comedy storyboard artist and performance specialist. Every character has a distinct, persistent personality that shapes how they think, move, interact, react, solve problems and behave around others. 1 PERSONALITY PROFILE: temperament, confidence, intelligence, competence, fears, motivations, emotional habits, strengths, weaknesses, quirks; kept across scenes unless the story changes them. 2 PERSONALITY-DRIVEN MOVEMENT: a calm expert moves deliberately and efficiently; a nervous beginner hesitates and overcorrects; an arrogant one exaggerates gestures; the overconfident amateur performs dramatically and makes obvious mistakes). 5 SOCIAL DYNAMICS AND STATUS: how characters see and treat each other (admiration, scepticism, rivalry, disrespect, friendship, jealousy, intimidation, shifting status), shown through glances, spacing, interruptions, body language and contrasting reactions. 6 CONTRAST COMEDY: calm expert among panicking onlookers, confident fool beside a worried genius, the underestimated one who succeeds. 7 HABITS AND MICRO-EXPRESSIONS matched to the individual (see CHARACTER ACTING). 8 REACTION CHOREOGRAPHY: supporting characters are directed as carefully as the lead: true eye lines, reaction delays, facial transitions, exchanged glances, group reactions, all based on what they actually see or know. 9 EMOTIONAL CONTINUITY: emotions develop through events; ## 8. Build fixes learned (magic bell, 2026-11-11). Each one cost a re-render; check them before rendering

## 10. THE PROVEN BUILD METHOD (pin stretch test, CB 2026-11-14: "Yes, rebuild film". Proof: episodes/armor-no-holes/stretches/armor-pins-test)
- A film is built in STRETCHES of one place each (10-25 s), one builder per stretch for the build, and a FRESH builder for every fix round who starts from the stretch's STATE.md (section 11, T2; changed 2026-11-15 because a builder kept for 13 hours cost 184 million tokens). Each stretch is ONE continuous take: the camera flies, lands, rests, flies again; no cut inside a stretch. Stretches join only where the place changes, through an object or a 8-12 frame blend.
- Every act of every character is an episode move written for that act (a moves file in the stretch's cast folder, loaded as `m:../../<file>/<style>`), drawn new on every frame: `tween.ones` true, `face_frames` 0. Count before any render: plain kit poses 0, frozen moves 0.
- Things that go in, on or at something FLY there and HIT: flight with a streak, a one-frame hit, a mark, a quiver, dust, and the thing that is hit shudders. Nothing just appears.
- Act frames are plain frame numbers taken from words.json, never shifted by a tool.
- Sheets made for every render and looked at by the builder AND the lead before CB: a strip at 4 frames per second, the LINE CHECK (each line's words beside its frames: do I see what it says?), feet and eyes zooms, a phone-size sheet.
- Still owed after the test, check on every stretch: nobody cut by the frame edge at any frame of a rest; floor at most a third of a wide; the small story thing (a pin, a chip, a chalk ring) large enough to read on a phone at the moment its word is said; props in hands as real 3D props where the kit can.
- Renders: full-size frames in parallel chunks (`episodes/armor-no-holes/stretches/armor-pins-test/render_final.sh` is the pattern). With several builders on one PC, at most 3 render processes per builder.
- CLOSE-UPS ON WHAT THE VOICE NAMES, AS A SECONDARY RULE THAT MUST MATCH THE SCENE (CB 2026-11-14 on the joined armor film: "there are some like close up missing and frame by narration", "When you say hole, you need to show a hole", "Zoom to a hole"; then, correcting the lead who made it the top rule: "It's not primary, it's secondary. It needs to match the scene."). The scene comes first: its staging, its acting, the flow of the take. Where it fits that scene, the camera flies in and lands close on the thing the voice names, on the word, rests a beat and flies on; a named part may be highlighted with an effect. Never force a close-up on every noun and never chop a take into a string of inserts.
- NOBODY CUT IN HALF, STANCES PHYSICALLY RIGHT (CB same day: "at second 9, he had removed by half", "when he stands on the ladder, it's like a wrong position"). Check every frame of a rest for a figure cut by the frame edge; anyone on a ladder, box or step has both soles on a visible tread and the body on the right side of it. Zoom on it before sending.
- HOW RICK DOES IT, MEASURED (4 films, 106 named things; study `episodes/armor-no-holes/refs/closeup_study/CLOSEUP_STUDY.md`, 2026-11-14): 96 % of named things are shown on their word, about 40 % close, 57 % in the scene view. A close-up is a push inside the running take (0.3-0.5 s in), the thing DOES something for about 1 s, then the camera pulls back or tilts to a face; a hand, tool or face is in it, light and place match the wide, the action never stops. A part is highlighted with the part itself (glow or colour running along it, glint, dust, a real marker); no outlines, arrows or floating labels, and the rest is not dimmed without a real cause. "Many of one thing" is revealed in under half a second with a hand placing the newest.
- RENDER ON THE GRAPHICS CARD (CB 2026-11-14: "Can you not use gpu all work?"). `templates/threejs_film/render_gpu.mjs` draws the three.js frames on the RTX 2070 SUPER: about 0.55-0.9 s per full-size frame against 4.7 s in software, picture equal apart from antialiased edges (tested on one film; `GPU_RENDER.md`). One process normally, two only when the card is free, never three, never while Blender uses the card. Never mix software and GPU frames inside one stretch. Check one frame per place both ways before a full run. Why the first try was black: a `pow()` of a slightly negative number in a set shader gives not-a-number on the card, and the bloom blur spread it; any new set shader must guard `pow`, `sqrt` and divisions.
- BLENDER LAYERS ON RUNPOD (CB 2026-11-14: "ok do runpod"). Blender layer frames render on rented RTX 4090 machines, not on our card: the picture matches local to 0.005 of 255, so cloud and local frames can share a shot. One Blender process is held up by the processor (tens of thousands of objects set per frame), so the speed comes from 6-8 processes per machine (4.2-4.6 s a frame against 23 s here) and from several machines at once. Steps and image in `episodes/armor-no-holes/blender_metal/RUNPOD.md` (needs `libegl1 libgl1 libopengl0`). Only the lead creates machines; check a machine's upload speed in the first 2 minutes and drop a slow one (one upload crawled at 18 MB in 20 minutes); terminate each machine the moment its frames are counted on this PC. Armor film: 362 metal frames, about 1 h 20 min of machine time, about 1.30 USD. Put the Blender layer only where the thing it improves covers at least 1.5 % of the picture (measured per frame on the matte); in wide shots it adds glints and nothing to see.

## 11. THE LEAN PIPELINE AND THE TOKEN RULES (CB 2026-11-15 after the armor film: "how we can optimized that is not burn too much tokes and quicker to make", "or what takes so much time and not worth", then "yes please do."). Wins over every older pipeline line in this file and in SKILL.md.
Why: the armor film (63.5 s) cost 1.66 billion tokens, 41 helpers and 22.7 working hours. Measured: about 750 million went into a still-picture storyboard with judge rounds that CB rejected when he saw it move; 97 % of all tokens were re-reading (every step a helper takes re-reads everything it has seen so far); builder 1 alone cost 184 million because one conversation ran 13 hours. Target for the next film of the same quality: at most 450 million tokens and 12 working hours.

### 11a. The lean pipeline (the order of work)
1. STORY AND VOICE, THEN THE PICTURE PLAN. One story unless CB asks for a choice; facts checked; voice recorded; `words.json`; then `PICTURE_PLAN.md` (11d): what the viewer sees on each word, written for this story.
2. THE ONE GATE: THE WHOLE FILM ROUGH, PLUS THE HOOK MOVING. As soon as script, voice and the first character are locked, one builder per stretch starts at once (section 10) with a cap of 60-90 minutes and brings 2-3 real three.js key frames for EVERY line of its stretch: no polish, stand-in cast allowed. The hook builder also brings its 10-15 s in motion with voice and captions. CB sees the picture plan, the whole board with its line check and the moving hook together, and says yes or no. Nothing is polished and no judge runs before this gate. Why both: the armor film showed that stills cannot prove motion or camera (CB rejected what the stills had passed); the Moonrakers film showed that polishing one scene for a day before CB has seen the whole film wastes the day (CB 2026-11-14: "til now i dint see the storybaord", then "Board all 20 lines now"). This replaces the hook-frame gate of section 0.
3. BUILD OUT AND POLISH THE STRETCHES from the approved board, in parallel (section 10), from ONE shared sets folder from the first day (never a copy of the sets per stretch), with the strong colours already in (11c). CB's approved board is the FLOOR: a polished frame keeps every story element its board panel shows (props, characters, the gag, the named thing) and the light of its chapter; the builder checks board beside polish before reporting.
4. CB'S NOTES IN BATCHES. The lead collects all notes on a version, runs the automatic checks first, then sends ONE fix round per version. Aim: two rounds.
5. SOUND, then THE GRADE as the last picture step, then delivery.
ONE FOLDER PER FILM (CB 2026-11-15: "never create so many subfolder. for one keep same name and create inside subfolder with version"). A film lives in ONE folder, `episodes/<name>/`, named after the film, never after a prompt or a task. Stretches, versions, fixes and tests go INSIDE it: `episodes/<name>/s2/`, `episodes/<name>/v3/`, `episodes/<name>/_tmp/`. Never a sibling folder such as `<name>-s2`, `<name>-s4a-raid`, `_ridefix_tmp_s4a` or `continue-<name>-from-handoff...`. Every helper brief names its subfolder inside the film folder. Temp folders are deleted when their stretch lands. At delivery the film folder keeps the final video, the scripts, the notes and the code; frame folders, old versions and logs are deleted. DELETE DIRECTLY, WITHOUT ASKING, as soon as the final is approved (CB 2026-11-15: "next time please delete directly after everything final keep only neccessary files and final files"): keep the final video(s), scripts, notes, code, cast, refs, sets and sound; delete every frame folder, old version, old animatic, log and temp folder. Then decode the kept final once to prove it plays.
Not in the pipeline any more, used only when CB asks for them by name:
- Department rounds on still pictures BEFORE the gate (camera, light, props and effects each sending fix rounds on a storyboard) and judge loops. On the armor film three such rounds chased judge scores of 5.5 and 6.8 and none of that work reached the film; on Moonrakers six judge and fix rounds went into 7.5 s while 18 lines were not started. A judge runs at most ONCE per version, AFTER the gate, on the moving clip and its sheets; never judge, fix, judge more than twice without CB seeing the version. The lead still LOOKS at the check sheets before CB sees anything (section 7 stays).
- A Blender layer. It cost about 80 million tokens and 6 hours for a gain that is hard to see on a phone. Use it only when a polished, glass or wet hero object is the star of the story, test it on 2 frames first, and put it only where that object covers at least 1.5 % of the picture.
- A repaint of finished sets. Colour is decided before the sets are built (11c).

### 11b. Token rules (every helper and the lead)
- T1 READ ONLY YOUR SLICE. A helper reads `rules/dept/<its department>.md` (made by `python tools/slice_rules.py`) and the files its brief names. It never reads the whole of CB_RULES.md or SKILL.md; when it needs one more rule it greps for the word. Only the lead reads the whole book, once per session.
- T2 A FRESH HELPER FOR EVERY FIX ROUND. A helper does one build or one fix round, then it ends. Before it reports it writes `STATE.md` in its work folder (at most 60 lines): what is built, the files and frame folders, the numbers measured, what is weak, what is open. The next round is a NEW helper that reads STATE.md and the notes. Never resume a helper that already did a round: each resumed step re-reads its whole history. (This replaces "one builder who stays through every fix round" in section 10.)
- T3 NUMBERS BEFORE PICTURES. Anything a script can measure is checked by a script that prints numbers: a figure cut by the frame edge, the named thing in frame on its word, floor share, saturation and brightness, frozen frames, loudness. A helper opens a picture only when a number fails or for the one final look. Pictures are looked at small: one sheet at most 1,600 px wide, at most 4 pictures per check round, never full-size frames one by one.
- T4 ONE COMMAND PER CYCLE. Render, sheets and measurements run from ONE script call. A long run goes in the background and the helper ends its turn; it does not wake up to wait, poll or look at a log. Every wake-up re-reads everything.
- T5 SHORT REPORTS, DETAIL IN FILES. A helper's report is at most 25 lines: done, numbers, weak points, files. Tables and lists go into a file the report names.
- T6 THE SMALL MODEL FOR MECHANICAL WORK. Rendering, joining, measuring, merging, fact checks, sound mix runs and file moves go to a helper on the smaller model (Agent tool `model: sonnet` or `haiku`). The main model is for story, acting, camera and look decisions.
- T7 A FRESH LEAD SESSION PER PHASE. Story, build and finish are three sessions, each started from HANDOFF.md. The lead never reads a helper's transcript and never pastes long tool output into the chat when a file path will do.
- T8 NOTHING BUILT TWICE. Before a helper builds a tool, a set part or a move, it greps `tools/`, `templates/` and the last episode for one that exists. Studies of Rick are done once and live in the rules and `references/`; a film does not study him again unless CB names a new question.
- T9 MEASURE EVERY FILM. At delivery the lead runs `python tools/token_report.py` and writes the totals into HANDOFF.md: tokens per helper, working hours, and the three most expensive helpers with the reason. A helper over 60 million tokens is a fault to explain.

### 11c. Colour from the first day
- The LOOK_BIBLE carries the measured targets before any set is built: saturation of the ungraded frames at least 0.36 for the whole film and 0.30 by day, a clearly different colour world per kind of light (distance between light kinds at least 18 before the grade), night frames never under mean brightness 0.31, one red in the film, faces white by day.
- Sets start from the armor film's coloured values (`episodes/armor-no-holes/colour_pass/COLOUR_PASS.md`: the pigment key `rich`, the light colours per place), not from grey defaults.
- The grade is the last picture step and is cheap (armor: 8 million tokens, 3 minutes for the whole film): start from `episodes/armor-no-holes/grade_final/` (per light kind, with the guards for reds, faces, caption letters and the ceiling).

### 11d. Solve it before it is built (CB 2026-11-15: "i want it have the problem solve in first place not to much fine tune", then "every story is different. it need to match to the story")
Why: of CB's 23 notes on the armor film about 14 were "the thing the voice names is not shown, not close enough or not highlighted", about 5 were "someone is cut off or stands wrong", about 4 were acting or a gag. Almost all were knowable before a frame was built.
- THE PICTURE PLAN, BEFORE ANY BUILD. After the script and voice the writer and the lead write `episodes/<name>/PICTURE_PLAN.md` in the format of `templates/PICTURE_PLAN.md`: one row for every thing or action the voice names, with the word it lands on, what we see, CLOSE / IN SCENE / NOT SHOWN and WHY in this story, the character's feeling on that word, an effect or gag only if this story's world gives one. CB approves it with the rough board (the one gate). Every row is a must for the builder; the line-check sheet is ordered by its rows.
- THE STORY DECIDES, NEVER A TEMPLATE. The plan is a format, not content: it is written fresh from each script and no row is copied from another film. A close-up goes in only where it fits the scene (section 10: secondary, must match the scene). "The voice says it" is not a reason; the reason is what the thing does in the story. Rick's numbers (96 % shown on the word, about 40 % close) check the total at the end and are never a quota per line.
- A LIBRARY OF PIECES IS TECHNIQUE, NOT CONTENT. Proven pieces live in `templates/pieces/` (built 2026-11-15 from the armor film: 17 pieces; 12 as code = hop, laugh, hit, double tap, live stroke, "got it", "told you so", the motion helpers, highlight of a part, fly-and-hit, burst of living things, time pass; 5 as numbers only = push-in, low-angle close-up, fly through an object, fly-land-rest, the "hundreds" count). Read `templates/pieces/INDEX.md`: each entry says WHEN IT FITS and when not, and what must change. `node templates/pieces/prove.mjs` and `prove_fx.mjs` must print PASS after any edit there. Known limits: the amounts of lean, arm and head inside a move are still the armor characters' and need an animator for another body; effect files give numbers per frame, the look is built new in the film's set module; the first film that imports a piece tests ONE move through the kit first (`templates/pieces/STATE.md`). A plan row names the piece AND what changes for this story (object, light, character). A piece used unchanged is generic and fails Hard Rule No. 2.
- AUTOMATIC FRAME CHECKS BEFORE CB SEES A VERSION: `node tools/frame_checks/check.mjs <film.json>` (built and proven 2026-11-15; about 40 s per stretch, quarter size, one process). On every camera rest it tests, per character: head with hat cut by the frame edge; the figure cut through by a side edge; set geometry between the camera and the face, or the head inside a mesh; feet floating or sunk. It prints numbers, saves one small crop per FAIL and exits 1 on a FAIL. Every builder runs it before reporting, and the lead before CB sees a version. A version with a FAIL is not sent, unless the picture-plan row says in words that the figure at the edge is meant (a CLOSE on a thing with a face at the edge); such a waiver is named in the report. Proof: it caught three planted faults (head cut 23 %, body cut 49 %, head inside the engine) and found the Chief's face cut in half at the edge of the chalk close-up in the approved armor film (S1 frames 184-205), which nobody had seen. Limits: three.js stage only, not a Blender pass; one character covering another is not reported; a moving character is exempt on that frame; tuned on two stretches of one film.
- A NOTE FROM CB IS CHECKED AGAINST THE PLAN. Was the row there? If not, the row is added to the film's plan, and if the kind of thing was missing from the format, `templates/PICTURE_PLAN.md` gets the line, so the next film's plan catches it.
