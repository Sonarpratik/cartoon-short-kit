# Physical staging and varied views

Saved user preferences for future shorts: use believable spatial construction, a designed sky, inhabited workplaces, varied camera azimuths and visible facial reactions. Retain the cartoon characters and painted materials.

- Rooms belong inside enclosing buildings, with floors, ceilings, full exterior walls and appropriately sized doors/windows. Do not expose an entire wall as a camera shortcut. A cutaway is a separate visual choice requiring the user's request. A camera travelling through a window must fit the real aperture throughout the crossing; inspect frames immediately inside, at and outside the wall.
- Build a control room as a workplace with a varied crew at separate stations. Distinguish a laboratory from its office: sample handling, monitoring instruments, bench services, record keeping, storage and communication equipment should explain the activity. Keep relevant hands, faces and instruments readable.
- Use three-quarter views around 25–45 degrees, selected side views, eye-level reactions and overhead establishing views where they explain the action. Never stage a character with their back to the camera or use over-the-shoulder shots ([character-design.md](character-design.md)). Front views are useful for readable instrument inserts; avoid making them the default for every room. Aim characters at their work or each other, using head turns, gaze and changing expressions rather than having every face look at the camera.
- Roads connect actual destinations: service yard → gate → access road → junction → city. Check continuity, turns, ground height and lane width. No arbitrary asphalt strip ending at a lawn, wall or factory interior. Keep people and benches on raised footpaths and cars within the road envelope.
- Keep foreground, middle and distant scale coherent. Use the sky, treelines, distant buildings and haze together; avoid a blue fog wall that erases geography. Day and night need different practical lights, window behaviour, shadows and visibility, while faces remain readable.

`templates/bridge/staging.ts` provides an enclosed shell, a connected road ribbon, a three-quarter camera helper and an aperture-crossing check. `templates/bridge/sky.ts` provides the procedural day/night sky and per-scene atmosphere used by the revised workplace shots. Copy these alongside the shared renderer into new projects; apply the root's environment settings in the player. The dimensions are editable staging defaults, not architectural standards. Keep story-specific geometry and camera keyframes local.

Crowds must look like different civilians: vary clothing colours, hair colour/style, headwear, facial hair, age, height and build as appropriate. Use deterministic individual appearances and distinct faces rather than repeating the same resident throughout a queue. Keep family members visually distinct and preserve coherent story-specific clothing.
