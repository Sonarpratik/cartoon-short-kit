# Reusable character views and facial motion

> On screen, characters stay within ±60° of facing the camera (±90° hard limit); see [character-design.md](character-design.md). The 12-view turntable below is for model-sheet review in Character Studio only. Idle micro-motion (breathing, weight shift, saccades, irregular blinks, hair follow-through) is built into the rig.

The user's persistent preference is a cast that can turn naturally, with lidded uneven eyes and small chin/cheek changes rather than a face frozen onto a front-facing body. The supplied images and private camera-reference video informed this preference. Do not redistribute those references or reproduce their characters.

Use [the shared library](../character-library/README.md), its original character designs and procedural rig for future projects. Save new designs and variants globally; copy selected cast definitions and the rig into each project before staging. Preserve each snapshot so future library changes do not change older projects.

Review a 12-view turntable (0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240, 270, 300, 330 degrees), top and bottom views, sitting, walking, bending, task poses and the expression range. Check the side silhouette, rear hair, far-arm occlusion, coat details and hidden facial features from behind. Derive view angles from world heading and camera elevation. A camera-facing card must not imply a front-facing drawing.

Facial motion should be gentle and reproducible from the timeline: seeded irregular blinking (`blinkLid`), gaze holds with quick saccades (`gazeShift`), expression-driven lids/brows/mouth, a damped head nod on expression change and low-amplitude chin width changes. Avoid random per-frame noise and whole-face scaling. The micro-expression switch in Character Studio allows comparing a neutral model sheet with the animated version.

On every expression change the head plays a short reaction (character-design.md §6): eyes squeeze shut for 2 frames, the head squashes wide with the jaw spreading most (+11 % big reactions, +7 % small), overshoots slightly tall, and settles within 0.6 s. It comes from `reactionBlink` / `reactionSquash` in the shared rig; do not add a second wobble on top.

The user's preferred face/body lighting has a soft glow on the key-light side and a visible darker form layer on the opposite side. Do not paint a white halo around the full face: it erases the shaded edge. The studio's Light selector allows checking both directions. Keep the off-white face and rounded facial features. For reactions, deform only the lower cheek/chin contour with expression-dependent width/height and a brief damped settle. Keep the upper head and body stable; neutral idle should not continuously jitter. Drive `fromExpression` and `expressionAge` from the timeline (seconds since the reaction), not wall-clock state, so review frames and exports agree.

Character Studio is a review tool, not a replacement for scene-specific animation. Match walk speed to travel, align hands to props, put sitting characters on seats, and review expressions and visible silhouettes through the actual camera.

For turn corrections, check hair/skull occlusion and shoulder/arm overlap at intermediate angles, not just the four cardinal views. In a top view the scalp belongs over the crown; do not translate the frontal fringe backward. Walks at 30° and 60° must use the same stance/swing timing and world-space feet as the full profiles. Review eight phases of each walk and compare frames immediately around 90°/270° to catch renderer switches. After QA, preserve the shared sources with `node character-library/preserve-shared.mjs`.

The user preferred the authored hair style over the 3D hair experiment, then requested separate designs at all 12 angles. `templates/bridge/angle-designs.ts` now defines those vector keys: scalp/fringe, temple locks, rear silhouettes and headwear change with the view, with matching components interpolated between keys. Receding hair stays at the temples instead of sliding across the forehead. Body keys control torso/shoulder width, front costume visibility and rear seams; side feet and limbs use the same world-space gait. Keep the preferred crown view, softer face and reaction animation. `hair-3d.ts` and `hair-models-3d.json` remain inactive experimental assets.

Run `node character-studio/save-turnarounds.mjs` after adding or revising a cast member. It saves each character's 12-view PNG and angle-design JSON under `projects/<project>/character-references/original/turnarounds/`. Review these through the studio's **Saved 12 angles** link, plus the live intermediate turns and actions, before preserving the shared renderer.

For faces, follow the private studies in `references/frames/` and the user's supplied examples: small rounded pupils beneath thin, slightly uneven lidded eye curves; rounded, gently curved eyebrows with round ends; compact curved or oval mouths. Avoid broad solid half-disc eyes and sharp tapered brows. Adapt expression-driven lids and mouth shapes while preserving our own head seed, face colour, body and outfit. Original completed-video design sources stay archived; shared studio previews record the revised face/hair style separately.

Hair, headwear, glasses and other head attachments must share the head transform, including gait sway, hip-driven height and local head tilt. Body accessories must share the torso transform. Use `upperBodyRotations` from `toon.ts`; do not draw attachments at unswayed head/body coordinates after `bodyFront` resets its transform. Check walking and running across all twelve angles with `node character-studio/check-head-attachments.mjs`.
